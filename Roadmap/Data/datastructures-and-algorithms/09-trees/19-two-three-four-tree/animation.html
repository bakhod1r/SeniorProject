2-3-4 Tree

A perfectly height-balanced search tree whose nodes are 2-nodes (1 key, 2 children), 3-nodes (2 keys, 3 children), or 4-nodes (3 keys, 4 children), with every leaf at the same depth. It is exactly a B-tree of order 4 (minimum degree t = 2) and is the structural twin of a red-black tree.

Ready. A 2-3-4 tree never lets a node hold more than three keys. Insertion descends top-down, pre-emptively splitting every full 4-node on the way down, so a key dropped into a leaf never has to cascade back up.

Insert splits any 4-node it meets while descending (its middle key rises into the guaranteed non-full parent), so the leaf insert is always safe — a root split grows the height. Delete also works top-down: before stepping into a 1-key child it tops the child up to ≥ 2 keys by borrowing from a 2+/3-node sibling (rotation through the parent) or fusing it with a sibling plus a parent key into a 4-node; an internal key is swapped with a leaf predecessor, and draining the root collapses one level. Toggle the red-black view to see the equivalent encoding: 2-node = black, 3-node = black + 1 red child, 4-node = black + 2 red children.
