Greedy list scheduling is (2−1/m)-competitive; sampling two servers and picking the lighter gives exponentially better balance

Setup — Online Makespan Minimization

Jobs arrive one at a time (online). Greedy list scheduling places each job on the currently least-loaded machine. The makespan is the load of the tallest machine. Offline OPT (best possible placement, computed here by exhaustive search) is the baseline. Theorem: greedy is (2 − 1/m)-competitive — it never exceeds (2 − 1/m)× OPT.

Setup — The Power of Two Choices

Throw n balls into n bins. With d = 1 (one random bin), the max load is about ln n / ln ln n. With d = 2 (sample two bins, drop into the lighter), the max load collapses to about ln ln n / ln 2 — an exponential improvement. This single change is why real load balancers sample a couple of servers instead of one.
